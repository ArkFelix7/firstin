### The blow-out graphic (closing visual)

A short, stylized illustration in flat vector style, using the product's palette (warm grays, one ink-blue accent, flame orange). No photos and no realistic fire, so it can't be mistaken for footage of a real incident.

1. **Frame 1, 0.0 s.** A firefighter in turnout gear, drawn simply, holds a tablet showing the FirstIn plan: the access-plan aerial with its green ladder walls is recognizable on the screen.
2. **Frame 2, 0.8 s.** In front of them is a small building model, about desk size. A flame sits on its roof, the size of a candle flame.
3. **Frame 3, 1.5 s.** The firefighter glances at the tablet, then leans in and blows, like blowing out a birthday candle.
4. **Frame 4, 2.3 s.** The flame bends and goes out, leaving a thin curl of smoke.
5. **Frame 5, 3.0 s.** The building model is intact. The firefighter lowers the tablet and the frame settles. The FirstIn wordmark and tagline fade in.

Tone notes:
- **Keep it calm and a little warm, not slapstick.** No cartoon cheeks or "whoosh" sound. If there's audio, it's one soft breath.
- **It's a metaphor for being prepared, not a claim that fires are easy.** If a judge reads it literally, the line under it does the correcting: "know the building before you're inside it."
- **Never use it near the opening.** It must come after the product demo and the business case, never next to the Houston story.

Fallback if there's no time to animate it: a single still of frame 4 (the flame going out, smoke curling) behind the final title card.

## Delivery notes

- **The first 26 seconds are slow and plain.** No music swell, no adjectives. The facts carry it.
- **Don't name the firefighters in the pitch** unless you're asked; it can read as using them. If a judge asks, they were Capt. Matthew Renaud, Engineer Operator Robert Bebee, Firefighter Robert Garner and Probationary Firefighter Anne Sullivan. Capt. Bill Dowling died of his injuries in 2017.
- **Never say the crew made a mistake.** The fire marshal found they did not fail to perform as trained.
- **Never say the roof tiles caused the collapse.** Say the crew had no record of how the roof was built.
- **If the live run is slow,** switch to the finished tab at 0:48 and keep talking. The trace replays from the log, so it looks the same.
- **The voice brief is the emotional turn.** Let it run. Silence from the speaker makes the room listen to it.

## Likely questions

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| What if the AI is wrong? | It never publishes without an officer. Every field shows its source. If there's no source, it says verify on site. It's a draft for the walk-through, not a replacement for it. |
| Doesn't First Due already do this? | First Due pre-fills parcel fields from assessor records. The interior, the roof and the tenants still need a site visit. We fill those, with citations, and we plug into First Due rather than replace it. |
| Most buildings don't have a Matterport scan. | Right. CoStar plus Vexcel still give construction, tenants, roof, access and exposures. A scan makes the interior section richer. |
| How fast? | About 30 seconds for a prepared building and about 50 seconds for any of the 3,288 live. |
| Does this reduce deaths? | No controlled study exists, and we won't claim one. What exists is a long run of NIOSH and fire marshal reports naming missing building information as a factor. |
