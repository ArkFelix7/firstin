# FirstIn: opening-story research (LA / Houston fires where building knowledge mattered)

Compiled 2026-11-15. Every fact below was checked against the cited source. Quotes in "double quotes" are verbatim. Where a primary report was available, I extracted its text directly (NIOSH and Texas SFMO PDFs) rather than relying on summaries. Items I could not verify are marked **UNVERIFIED**.

---

## TL;DR recommendation

**Lead: Houston, Southwest Inn / Bhojan restaurant fire, May 31, 2013.** It's the strongest causal link of any candidate. Both official investigations explicitly name the lack of a pre-plan:

- Texas State Fire Marshal, **Finding 1** (Investigation FFF FY 13-08): *"There were no pre-fire plans of this structure. The Houston Fire Department had responded to this location in the past but there is no indication of a record of a site diagram, fire protection systems, or construction methods."*
- NIOSH **F2013-16**: *"At this incident, there was no information available for first due or first alarm companies due to the fact this occupancy was not considered a high-risk occupancy."* and *"This incident had considerations unknown to the responding fire officers and fire fighters that caused a collapse of the structure."* Recommendation #4 is a pre-incident planning program (NFPA 1620), and Recommendation #5 is a "critical building information system" available to responding units.

Each field FirstIn fills in maps to a specific failure in this incident: renovations and re-roofs (permits without inspections), concrete tiles added over 3 layers of roofing, lap-nailed wood trusses, a Type V wood-frame restaurant attached to a motel, a commercial kitchen tenant, and no sprinklers.

**Alternate: Charleston Sofa Super Store, June 18, 2007** (not LA/Houston, but it's the clearest "the pre-plan existed and was inadequate" story, and it comes with a scannable artifact: NIOSH printed the department's handwritten pre-plan form in Appendix III). **Best LA/Houston alternate: LA I-10 freeway pallet-yard fire, Nov 11, 2023.** No deaths, but it's a large and recent event with documented hidden fuel load (pallets, hand sanitizer, solvents) that a state inspector had flagged a year earlier.

**Suggested respectful opener (about 15 seconds):**
> "On May 31st, 2013, four Houston firefighters went into a burning restaurant on the Southwest Freeway, and the roof came down twelve minutes after the first engine arrived. The state's investigation found, in its first finding, that there was no pre-fire plan: no site diagram, no record of the construction. FirstIn exists so the next crew knows the building before they get there."

(Don't name the firefighters on stage unless you're also honoring them. If you do, use full ranks: Capt. Matthew Renaud, Engineer Operator Robert Bebee, Firefighter Robert Garner, Probationary Firefighter Anne Sullivan, and Capt. William "Bill" Dowling, who died in 2017 of his injuries.)

### What building knowledge would have addressed
The responding officers didn't know about the lightweight lap-nailed trusses, the stacked re-roofs, the added tile weight, the attic void above a commercial kitchen, or the unpermitted modifications. They also couldn't see Side Charlie: "The first units to be assigned to Side Charlie were during RIT Operations … approximately 20 minutes into the incident" (NIOSH p.49).

**Nuance (be accurate on stage):** The SFMO report says the city's engineering firm concluded *"The roof failure was determined to be the result of fire exposure and not a failure of the construction method or weight of the roof."* However, SFMO Finding 1 itself says the tile *"added several pounds per square foot to the roof's weight … and the weight of the roof shortened the time from the incipient stages of the fire to the time of collapse."* Don't claim "the tiles caused the collapse." Say instead: "the crew had no record of how the roof was built."

### Verbatim investigator findings

**NIOSH F2013-16** ("4 Career Fire Fighters Killed and 16 Fire Fighters Injured at Commercial Structure Fire – Texas", July 8, 2015; revised Apr 5, 2017 and Dec 3, 2021)
- Contributing factors: "Fire burning unreported for 3 hours; Delayed notification of the fire department; **Building construction**; Wind impacted fire; **Scene size-up**; Personnel accountability; Fireground communications; Lack of fire sprinkler system."
- p.14: "This incident had considerations unknown to the responding fire officers and fire fighters that caused a collapse of the structure."
- p.14: "A thorough understanding of building construction and the effects of fire dynamics within a renovated building is necessary for the officers to correctly complete an initial size-up, assess building integrity and formulate applicable strategies."
- p.49: "Based upon the type of occupancy, the department did not pre-plan this building based on the risk assessment."
- **Recommendation #4:** "Fire departments should consider implementing a pre-incident planning program which complies with NFPA 1620, Standard for Pre-Incident Planning." Its discussion lists what a plan should hold. This is essentially FirstIn's output schema: "A site plan; Floor plans; Construction type; age and condition of the building; Ingress and egress; Pre-existing structural damage/deterioration; … Engineered load systems/lightweight construction; … Roof construction and covering including HVAC units; Renovation/modifications to structure; Height of the building; Fuel loads; Fire protection features such as sprinkler systems, standpipe system, fire alarm system, and hydrant locations; Stairwells; Utility shut-offs; Occupant contact information".
- p.55: "Pre-incident information, pre-fire plans and building knowledge are mission critical - at a minimum for the first-due area response."
- p.55: "At this incident, there was no information available for first due or first alarm companies due to the fact this occupancy was not considered a high-risk occupancy."
- p.55: "a jurisdiction's building and permit office or department may have information that the fire department does not have." (This supports FirstIn's data-fusion pitch.)
- **Recommendation #5:** "Fire departments should consider implementing a critical building information system which is available to responding units to enhance situational awareness." Examples given: "bowstring truss, major alterations have occurred," and "Hazardous chemicals, liquids and substances. This information should always indicate floor and location of these hazards".
- Recommendation #15: "States and municipalities should consider adopting and enforcing regulations for automatic fire sprinkler protection in renovated structures."
- 15 recommendations total.

**Texas State Fire Marshal's Office, Firefighter Fatality Investigation FFF FY 13-08** (released ~Oct/Nov 2014)
- **Finding 1:** "There were no pre-fire plans of this structure. The Houston Fire Department had responded to this location in the past but there is no indication of a record of a site diagram, fire protection systems, or construction methods."
- Recommendation: "Pre-fire tactical planning can be employed to identify lightweight truss construction to assist in formulating interior firefighting tactics … Visiting target hazards that have undergone building modifications will give primary response companies knowledge that may impact interior firefighting tactics and strategy." and "Identifying modifications to roof structures … should be included as a primary segment of pre-incident tactical planning."
- Finding 2: "Initial entry crews did not perform a 360° scene size-up."
- Finding 3: "Firefighters were not aware of the severity of the fire conditions present overhead and that structural members of the roof support system had become compromised."
- Exoneration (important for tone): "The final analysis of this incident does not suggest that the firefighters who lost their lives, or any of the surviving members of the Houston Fire Department, failed to perform their duties as trained or as expected by their organization."
- 8 findings total.

**Pitch framing:** this is a pre-plan failure, not a crew failure. Both reports put the gap at the system level, in what wasn't written down. HFD itself disputed that the listed failures caused the deaths (per Firefighter Nation coverage), so keep the claim at "the crew didn't have the building's information," not "a pre-plan would have saved them."

Sources: NIOSH https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/163331/cdc_163331_DS1.pdf · SFMO https://www.tdi.texas.gov/reports/fire/documents/fmloddhouston2013.pdf · NBC https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/cause-blaze-killed-four-houston-firefighters-under-investigation-flna6C10149696 · Click2Houston (NIOSH) https://www.click2houston.com/news/2015/07/21/final-report-on-southwest-inn-fire-finds-eight-contributing-factors · Click2Houston (Dowling) https://www.click2houston.com/news/2017/03/08/capt-bill-dowling-firefighter-injured-in-2013-southwest-inn-fire-dies-at-43

## 2. Los Angeles: I-10 freeway pallet-yard fire, Nov 11, 2023

- **Date/place:** Nov 11, 2023; storage yards beneath the elevated I-10 near downtown LA (Alameda St to East LA interchange).
- **Deaths/injuries:** none reported in the sources reviewed.
- **What burned:** AP: "pallets, cars, construction materials, hand sanitizer and other items being stored under the freeway"; "burned about 100 support columns"; "An estimated 300,000 vehicles use the stretch of freeway daily" (AP, Christopher Weber, Nov 17, 2023).
- **Cause:** CAL FIRE: arson ("CAL FIRE believes the fire was caused by arson" per Caltrans release).
- **Closure:** Reopened the night of Nov 19, 2023, eight days later and weeks ahead of the original 3–5 week estimate (Caltrans news release 2023-042).
- **Occupancy/tenant:** Land leased from Caltrans by Apex Development Inc. since 2008 and sublet to at least six other companies (AP, via news). The lease prohibited flammable/hazardous materials.
- **Building-knowledge angle (strong for FirstIn's "palletized storage" flag):** AP (Stefanie Dazio, Nov 19, 2023): "State inspectors have visited the site six times since early 2020 and flagged problematic conditions for years." In a 2022 inspection, a Caltrans inspector wrote "This is a filthy unmaintained lease" after finding "solvents, oils, fuels and other items prohibited by the agreement."
- **Cost:** **UNVERIFIED.** I found no authoritative repair-cost figure. Don't quote a dollar number.
- **Investigator report:** No NIOSH/after-action report (no FF fatality). The evidence is the state's inspection records reported by AP.
- **Fit:** good for a "hidden fuel load / unknown occupancy" beat, weak on human stakes. Use it as the second beat ("and it's not just old buildings").

Sources: https://dot.ca.gov/news-releases/news-release-2023-042 · https://komonews.com/news/nation-world/speedy-repairs-to-arson-fire-damaged-la-los-angeles-freeway-promise-early-reopening-governor-gavin-newsom-interstate-10-pallets-cars-construction-materials-sanitizer-arrests · https://www.fireengineering.com/fire-safety/investigators-found-fire-safety-hazards-freeway-arson/ · https://www.everand.com/article/683928155/10-Freeway-In-Downtown-L-A-Shut-Down-After-Fire-Newsom-Declares-State-Of-Emergency (LA Times headline, Ruben Vives, Nov 12 2023, paywalled preview)

## 3. Houston: Watson Grinding explosion, Jan 24, 2020

- **Primary source:** U.S. Chemical Safety Board, "Fatal Propylene Release and Explosion", Report No. 2020-03-I-TX, released June 29, 2023.
- **Date:** CSB report: "Shortly before 4:30 a.m. on January 24, 2020" (Note: the CSB *press release* says Jan 23. That's a typo; the report says Jan 24 and gives Jan 23 as the prior day's shutdown.)
- **Deaths/injuries:** "fatally injured two employees and injured two additional employees. A nearby resident was also fatally injured." Total 3 dead.
- **Damage:** "over 450 structures were affected by the explosion, with some sustaining major damage."
- **Cause:** "degraded and poorly crimped rubber welding hose that disconnected from its fitting inside a coating booth"; manual shutoff valve left open; gas detection/shutoff inoperative.
- **CSB safety issues:** "lack of a comprehensive process safety management program" and "ineffective emergency response plan."
- **Building-knowledge angle:** The industrial building sat within a few hundred feet of homes and stored a flammable gas. The CSB focuses on the facility's process safety, **not** on fire-department pre-planning. There's no documented "responders didn't know" finding, so don't claim one.
- **Fit:** dramatic, but the causal link to FD pre-plans is weak. Use only as a "hazmat next to houses" visual.

Sources: https://www.csb.gov/assets/1/6/Watson_Final_Report_2023-06-29.pdf · https://www.csb.gov/csb-releases-final-report-on-2020-fatal-propylene-release-and-explosion-at-the-watson-grinding-facility-in-houston-texas/

## 4. Houston-area chemical fires (context)

- **ITC Deer Park, March 17, 2019:** CSB Report 2019-01-I-TX (July 2023). A circulation-pump failure on Tank 80-8 released butane-enriched naphtha. The fire spread to 14 other tanks, burned about 3 days, and caused more than $150M property damage (per CSB summary via search; I did not open the full PDF). No deaths. https://www.csb.gov/csb-releases-final-report-on-2019-massive-tank-farm-fire-at-the-intercontinental-terminals-company-facility-in-deer-park-texas/
- **KMCO Crosby, April 2, 2019:** isobutylene release from a failed y-strainer; 1 worker killed (James "Bubba" Mangum per news). Injury counts vary (2 critical in early reports; CSB factual update cites company records of 7 employee injuries). https://www.csb.gov/assets/1/6/CSB_KMCO_Factual_Update.pdf
- **Fit:** industrial process-safety stories, not building pre-plans. Not recommended as the lead.

## 5. Los Angeles: First Interstate Bank fire, May 4, 1988

- **Primary source:** USFA Technical Report 022, "Interstate Bank Building Fire, Los Angeles, California (May 4, 1988)", J. Gordon Routley/TriData. Mirror: https://lafire.com/famous_fires/1988-0504_1stInterstateFire/FEMA-TecReport/FEMA-report.htm
- **Facts (from search summaries of USFA/NIST, not read in full):** 62-story tower; fire destroyed 4 floors (12–16) and damaged a 5th; **1 death** (a maintenance worker sent up by security, found in the elevator); ~14 firefighters injured (counts vary). Built 1973, before LA's high-rise sprinkler ordinance. A sprinkler retrofit was ~90% complete but not operational; NIST notes standpipe system had been temporarily shut off. Cause likely electrical, not definitive. **Treat these as PARTIALLY VERIFIED.**
- **Fit:** famous high-rise systems story (sprinklers/standpipes), but the lesson is "install sprinklers," not "know the building." It's old and the causal link to pre-plans is weak.

## 6. Other Houston / LA commercial line-of-duty deaths with NIOSH reports

- **Houston McDonald's fire, Feb 14, 2000: NIOSH F2000-13**, "Restaurant Fire Claims the Life of Two Career Fire Fighters – Texas" (pub. Feb 7, 2001). 2 HFD firefighters killed (Lewis Mayo, 44, and Kimberly Smith, 30, the first female HFD LODD, per news; NIOSH does not name them) when the roof section over the kitchen collapsed. NIOSH recommends training "to identify truss roof systems," caution on/under lightweight truss roofs, and "exterior construction placards" by owners. It's a strong precedent: the same failure mode at a Houston restaurant 13 years before Southwest Inn. PDF: https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/163909/cdc_163909_DS1.pdf
- **Los Angeles, March 8, 1998: NIOSH F98-07**, "Commercial Structure Fire Claims the Life of One Fire Fighter – California". LAFD Engine 57 Captain (named Dupee on lafire.com's archive path) died in a one-story commercial building (a dog-treat preparation operation, 110 × 59 ft) with a "wooden, arched-truss roof" that partially collapsed. **The report does not mention pre-plans**, and its recommendations cover size-up, accountability, RIT and communications. https://lafire.com/lastalarm_file/1998-0308_Dupee/1998-0308_NIOSH-REPORT/NioshReport.htm
- **"HFD Captain Bill Dunbar": UNVERIFIED / likely a mix-up.** I found no such LODD. The prompt likely meant **Capt. William "Bill" Dowling** (Southwest Inn, died 2017).
- The NIOSH searchable list post-2017 lives at https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/fire/ (the index didn't render for automated fetch). Pre-2017 reports are on stacks.cdc.gov.

## 7. January 2025 LA wildfires (Palisades / Eaton): context only

- AP one-year retrospective (via PBS): 16,246 structures destroyed (Cal Fire); 31 deaths (19 Eaton, 12 Palisades). Per-fire structure counts differ slightly by source (ABC7, citing Cal Fire: Eaton 9,414; Palisades 6,837).
- **Fit: doesn't fit.** These were wind-driven wildland-urban-interface conflagrations where structure-by-structure pre-plans weren't the limiting factor (wind, water supply, and scale were). Tying them to FirstIn would look opportunistic to judges. At most, use one line: "LA just rebuilt 16,000 structures; the next generation of buildings deserves pre-plans from day one." **I recommend leaving it out.**

## 8. Charleston Sofa Super Store, June 18, 2007: README fact-check

Primary: NIOSH **F2007-18**, "Nine Career Fire Fighters Die in Rapid Fire Progression at Commercial Furniture Showroom – South Carolina," dated Feb 11, 2009. https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/163618 (PDF: https://stacks.cdc.gov/view/cdc/163618/cdc_163618_DS1.pdf)

| README claim | Verdict |
|---|---|
| 9 firefighters killed | **VERIFIED**: "nine career fire fighters (all males, ages 27 – 56) died" |
| NIOSH F2007-18 | **VERIFIED** |
| 43 recommendations incl. pre-incident planning | **VERIFIED**: Recommendations #1–#43. #16: "Fire departments should conduct pre-incident planning inspections of buildings within their jurisdictions to facilitate development of safe fireground strategies and tactics." #18: "develop and coordinate pre-incident planning protocols with mutual aid departments". #32 includes "Fire departments should use pre-incident planning and building inspections to identify structures within their jurisdiction that contain truss construction." |
| Quote: pre-plan "did not note any unusual hazards" | **NOT FOUND in the final NIOSH report** (full-text search found no such phrase; "unusual" appears only in generic NFPA guidance). It may come from a draft or another report (city's Routley review or NIST), but I couldn't locate it. **Replace it in README.md and DEVPOST.md.** |

**Verbatim replacement quotes (from the final report):**
- p.18: "A building pre-plan form obtained from the fire department dated April 26, 2006 noted that store contents were "household furniture and office equipment" and that the rear warehouse contained racks approximately 30 feet high … **The pre-plan form did not mention the large volume of furniture and flammable materials (fuel load) contained in the structure.**"
- p.39 (Rec. #5 discussion): "**Pre-plan information did not identify the potential hazards associated with the lightweight metal roof trusses, and the excessive fuel loads associated with the contents.**"
- p.48: "A more thorough building inspection and pre-incident plan for this single-story commercial building could have potentially identified the flat roof supported by lightweight metal bar joists (metal roof trusses), the immense fuel load considerations … the presence of a drop ceiling and hydrant locations."

**Building:** "one-story, commercial furniture showroom and warehouse facility totaling over 51,500 square feet that incorporated mixed-construction types. The structure was non-sprinklered." Originally a 1960s 17,500 sq ft grocery store, expanded several times. It was treated as 4 separate structures and so exempt from the sprinkler requirement.

**Visual asset:** NIOSH Appendix III (pp.89–90) reproduces the department's actual **handwritten 2006 pre-plan form**: Type of Roof "Comp.", Sprinkler "N/A", Contents "House hold furniture + office equipment". Next to a FirstIn-generated plan, it makes a strong before/after slide (public-domain federal document).

Suggested README wording: *"After the 2007 Charleston Sofa Super Store fire killed nine firefighters, NIOSH found the department's pre-plan form "did not mention the large volume of furniture and flammable materials (fuel load)" and did not identify "the lightweight metal roof trusses" (NIOSH F2007-18)."*
