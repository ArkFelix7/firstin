### 1.5 Grants and other funding

- **FEMA Fire Prevention & Safety (FP&S), FY2024:** $32.4M available, about 100 awards, up to $1.5M federal share each. The Code Enforcement/Awareness category lists "**Support for conducting inspections or pre-planning (including personnel costs, software, supplies, and training assistance)**" as an eligible project. **This is the right grant.** [P][27]
- **FEMA AFG, FY2024:** $291.6M available, about 2,000 awards. In the equipment section, "CAD systems and software, geographic information systems (GIS)" and "**Subscriptions, memberships…**" are listed as **ineligible**, except subscriptions needed to operate funded equipment. So **pre-planning SaaS looks like a poor fit for AFG Operations & Safety**. Don't claim AFG eligibility. [P][28]
- **E911 surcharge funds:** Washoe County (NV) pays for First Due pre-planning out of 911 telephone surcharges, on the grounds that pre-plan data gets "information to the responders." This precedent can be reused in other states with similar statutes. [P][5][6]

## 2. Who else pays

### 2.1 ISO / Verisk Public Protection Classification (PPC)

Verified from the text of an ISO PPC Summary Report [P][29]:
- ISO has classified **39,000+ fire protection areas**. "Insurance companies use PPC information for marketing, underwriting, and to help establish fair premiums for homeowners and commercial fire insurance… the price of fire insurance in a community with a good PPC grade is **substantially lower**."
- FSRS weights: Emergency Communications 10, **Fire Department 50**, Water Supply 40, Community Risk Reduction 5.5. Each PPC class spans **10 points** (Class 1 = 90+).
- Under Fire Department, **Training is worth 9 points**. Training sub-item **H, "Pre-Fire Planning Inspections," is 12 of the 100 training credits**: "For maximum credit, pre-fire planning inspections of each commercial, industrial, institutional, and other similar type building (all buildings except 1-4 family dwellings) should be made **annually** by company members. Records of inspections should include up-to-date notes and sketches."
- Example: Little Lake FD (CA) earned **0.00 of 12**, and Estes Valley FPD (CO) earned **0.48 of 12** [P][29][30].

**Math (honest):** full pre-planning credit = 12/100 × 9 = **1.08 FSRS points** out of about 105.5. That alone won't move a typical community a whole class, but it can tip a community sitting near a band edge. **Caveat:** ISO credits *inspections by company members* with records. An AI draft alone earns nothing. Pitch FirstIn as what makes the **annual walk-through of every commercial building** feasible: the draft plus the VERIFY ON SITE checklist turns a blank-page survey into a confirmation pass.

Premium savings in dollars per class change: **[U]**. Verisk does not publish a schedule, and it varies by insurer and state.

### 2.2 Insurers and risk engineering

- US commercial property direct premiums written (allied lines + fire + CMP non-liability): **$103.97B in 2024** [S][31] (S&P Global Market Intelligence).
- Vexcel's own release says its imagery supports "**insurance underwriting**, catastrophe response…" [P][12]. Insurers already buy this data layer.
- FM (FM Global): headcount of 1,500–2,000+ engineers is **[U]** (conflicting secondary figures). Industry-wide risk-engineering spend is **[U]**; we found no public total. Don't size it on the slide.

### 2.3 Building owners and property managers (CoStar's customers)

CoStar sells to owners, brokers and lenders. Owners already must host annual fire inspections. California H&S Code §13146.2 requires annual inspections of apartments with 3+ units, per Long Beach FD's FAQ ([S]). An owner-shared, cited pre-plan is useful to them for insurer submissions and fire-marshal visits. Willingness to pay is **[U]**.

### 2.4 NFPA 1660 (2024): what it says

- NFPA 1660 (2024) consolidated NFPA 1600, 1616 and **1620 (pre-incident planning)**. The pre-incident planning material is in **Chapters 17–23**, with Chapter 23 covering **plan maintenance**: 23.1 General, 23.2 Pre-Incident Plan Update, 23.3 Site Liaison [P][32][33].
- A.17.2.1: "a pre-incident plan is one of the most valuable tools available for aiding responding members in effectively controlling an emergency." Quoted by NIOSH in F2023-07 [P][34].
- Annex A.21.2 says plans for hazmat facilities should record the impact on operations, the specific hazards, quantities and container types, engineering controls, containment, suppression systems, and special firefighting requirements [P][32].
- The plan should cover building construction, materials, occupancy, fuel load, roof and floor design, protection systems and water supply, hydrants, and special hazards. If possible it should be "entered into the dispatcher's computer" [P][34] (NIOSH paraphrasing NFPA 1660).
- **Which occupancies must have plans, and how often to update them: [U].** We could not get the normative text of Ch. 23 because NFPA requires a login. The annual cadence that **is** verified is ISO's (see 2.1). Say "ISO credits annual pre-fire planning inspections of every commercial building", not "NFPA requires annual updates".

### 3.4 Evidence that pre-planning matters (NIOSH)

- **NIOSH F2025-09 (March 2026), Georgia warehouse / unpermitted film studio, Sept 8, 2025:** a master firefighter died after becoming **disoriented** in a commercial warehouse. "**Pre-incident planning**" is listed as a contributing factor. Recommendation #8: "Fire departments should develop a pre-incident plan for high-risk occupancies such as warehouses." The report states: "**no pre-incident plan was available for crews to view enroute or on arrival**… These pre-incident plans were kept in **three-ring binders** on each apparatus." [P][40]
- **NIOSH F2023-07 (April 2025), South Carolina:** a 25-year-old firefighter died in the **collapse of a lightweight-construction** apartment building. Pre-incident planning is a contributing factor, and Recommendation #11 is to develop a pre-incident plan for high-risk occupancies. The report says: "**Because many fire departments are unable to pre-plan for all the structures within their jurisdiction**, departments may opt to prioritize…" and "**The use of technology and programs can assist fire departments in conducting pre-incident planning**…" [P][34]
- **Charleston Sofa Super Store, June 18, 2007:** 9 firefighters died after becoming disoriented. NIOSH F2007-18 (Feb 2009) made **43 recommendations** [S][3][4].
- **How often NIOSH recommends pre-planning: [U].** We found no published count. Kunadharaju et al. (2011) reviewed 189 NIOSH investigations from 2004–09, which produced 1,167 recommendations. "Personnel management and planning/incident command" made up about 70% of high-frequency recommendations in trauma cases, but pre-planning was not isolated [S][41]. A count could be done by hand from the NIOSH FFFIPP index (a good post-hackathon task).
- **Controlled outcome studies showing that pre-plans reduce injuries: none found.** The evidence is case-based (NIOSH) and expert consensus (NFPA 1660, ISO credit). Say so if judges ask.

## 4. Market sizing (conservative; every assumption labeled)

### Inputs (verified)
- US commercial buildings: **5,918K** (CBECS 2018; excludes manufacturing) [P][42]. Of these, **1,727K are 10,000+ sq ft** (981+386+218+93+40+9) and **746K are 25,000+ sq ft**.
- Departments protecting 100K+: **431**. 25K–100K: **1,872**. 10K–25K: 3,512 [P][15].
- Observed spend on a full First Due suite: **$27.6K–$58K/yr** for mid-size cities [P][5][6][14].

### TAM: US fire service, pre-plan data layer
- **Per building:** 5.918M commercial buildings × **$10/building/yr** (assumption: annual auto-draft plus refresh) = **$59M/yr**.
- **Per department (cross-check):** 431 × $30K + 1,872 × $10K + 3,512 × $3K = $12.9M + $18.7M + $10.5M = **$42M/yr**. Assumption: an add-on priced at 25–70% of what a department already pays for its whole suite.
- → **US fire-service TAM ≈ $40–60M/yr.** This is a real but niche market. Say so.

### SAM: large career departments, plans for buildings 10K+ sq ft
- 1,727K buildings × $15/building/yr (assumption) ≈ **$26M/yr**. Equivalently, the 431 departments protecting 100K+ at $30K ≈ **$13M**, plus the 1,872 departments protecting 25K–100K at $10K ≈ **$19M** → about **$32M**.

### SOM: 3-year target (assumptions)
- 40 direct departments × $20K = $0.8M, plus **one platform API deal** (e.g., ImageTrend/FlowMSP, ~500 FlowMSP customers [S][43]) with 300 agencies × $4K = $1.2M → **≈ $2M ARR**.

### API licensing to platforms
Platforms already own the CAD-linked workflow: First Due (3,000+ agencies [S][9]), ImageTrend + FlowMSP (~500 pre-plan customers [S][43]), Tablet Command (~700 agencies, Aug 2023 [S][44]), ESO (7,500+ agencies via Emergency Reporting, 2021 [S][45]). Wholesale price per generated plan: **[U]**, so model it as a revenue share.

### Upside beyond the fire service (label as upside, don't add to TAM)
- Commercial property insurance: **$104B** in 2024 direct premiums [S][31]. Insurer spend on property inspections and risk engineering: **[U]**. Illustration only: if 0.1% of premium went to building-level hazard data, that would be about $104M/yr. **Do not present this as a market size.**
- For CoStar, the larger opportunity is new **fields and products** on its existing property records. CoStar says it plans to extract "hundreds of data fields per property" across about **half a billion properties** with AI after the Vexcel deal [P][12].

## 6. CoStar strategic angle

- **Matterport:** closed Feb 28, 2025. $1.6B EV at announcement; $1.9B total purchase consideration under GAAP. **14M+ spaces and 50B sq ft digitized** in 177 countries [P][10][11].
- **Vexcel:** agreement announced **Oct 27, 2026** for about **$730M cash**, funded from cash and the revolver. **Expected close Q1 2027**, pending HSR and foreign approvals. Vexcel has **>$140M LTM revenue, up >30%**, ~20% adj. EBITDA margin, 7.5–15 cm imagery, **99.6% US population coverage**, 320 metros, US metros refreshed up to 3×/yr, disaster zones flown within 24 hours. It already serves **insurance underwriting** and catastrophe response [P][12][13].
- **Why this is a new product line:** once Vexcel closes, CoStar will hold building interiors (Matterport), exteriors and roofs (Vexcel) and occupants (CoStar tenant records). Fire pre-plans are a natural output that neither real-estate data vendors nor fire-software vendors can produce alone:
  1. **Public safety:** a data feed or API sold into First Due, ImageTrend, ESO and Tyler, which already have the distribution. Departments pay $27K–$58K/yr for those suites.
  2. **Insurers:** the same cited hazard and construction fields support underwriting. Vexcel already sells to this buyer.
  3. **Owners:** a shareable "fire-ready" building profile for CoStar and LoopNet listings and for insurer submissions.
- Phrase it as: "CoStar **will** own the inside, outside and occupants of the same building once the Vexcel deal closes."

## 7. Recommended buyer and pricing story

- **Who pays:** large career departments (431 protect 100K+ people; career departments cover 70% of the US population). They buy through the pre-plan platform they already use, so lead with an **API into First Due / ImageTrend-FlowMSP / Tablet Command**, with direct sales as the fallback.
- **How it's funded:** FEMA **FP&S** grants name "pre-planning… software" as eligible. E911 surcharge funds already pay for pre-planning in Washoe County. The ISO training item gives 12 of 100 points for **annual** pre-fire planning inspections of every commercial building, a cadence that is impossible to meet by hand and that FirstIn makes realistic. The NERIS (Jan 1, 2026) software-replacement cycle opens the door.
- **Price:** about **$10–15 per commercial building per year**, capped at roughly $10K–$30K per department. That is well under the $27K–$58K departments already pay for the whole suite, and it gives a US fire-service TAM of about **$40–60M/yr**. Insurers and owners are upside for CoStar, not part of the core sizing.
